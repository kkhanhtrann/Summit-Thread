# Summit Thread design context

## Audience

Conference attendees who may have difficulty hearing, need more time to process technical language, arrive after a talk begins, speak another language, or prefer not to interrupt publicly. Presenters need a separate, simple demo control surface.

## Main jobs

- Read the speaker's actual words while listening.
- Search and revisit an earlier passage without losing the current reading position.
- Join a presenter-created live room by code and follow finalized speech as it is released.
- Watch or listen to the shared conference source beside the current topic thread.
- Create a fresh room, share its invite link or code, prepare a source, and start the live session.

## Product tone

Friendly, clear, and modern, never noisy. The media, current topic, and words sit together; controls stay quiet until needed. Explain room state plainly in sentence case.

## Visual direction

Follow the logo: a teal mountain line, an amber sun, and an amber thread. Use the tokens in `web/src/shared/styles/tokens.css`; see `docs/features/frontend-design.md`.

- Light cream ground (`#FBF7F0`), warm-white panels (`#FFFDF9`), and espresso ink (`#2A221B`). No green, teal, blue, or grey backgrounds or text.
- A coffee-brown top bar (`#5A3420`) spans the full width of every screen.
- One dark espresso block per screen (`#2E241C`) gives contrast: the invite panel, the video panel, your questions in Ask.
- Amber (`#FBBF24`) means "now": live status, the newest caption, the current topic, and the main step into a talk. Ember (`#8A4B00`) is its text shade and the focus color. Do not spend amber on decoration.
- Logo teal stays in the logo, the entry drawing, and the empty-topic trail. Topic threads get warm accents (teal, coral, tangerine, pink) on markers and edges only.
- Bricolage Grotesque for titles, the wordmark, and room codes; Source Sans 3 for reading and UI.
- Rounding follows hierarchy (panels 14px, cards 12px, controls 12px, chips 8px, buttons pill). Shadows are tinted with espresso.
- One orchestrated motion moment: the entry screen draws the logo once. Everything else animates only in response to an action or a new event, and all motion respects reduced-motion settings.

Copy speaks to customers, not engineers: short benefit-first headlines, verb-first buttons, and no subtitle that restates a heading or explains how the system works.

Avoid all-caps tracked labels, `A · B` meta strings, identical card grids, gradient washes as decoration, and raw logo colors as text on light surfaces. Preserve clear keyboard focus, screen-reader names, reduced motion, and spacing overrides.
