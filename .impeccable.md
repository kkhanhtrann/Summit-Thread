# Summit Thread design context

## Audience

Conference attendees who may have difficulty hearing, need more time to process technical language, arrive after a talk begins, speak another language, or prefer not to interrupt publicly. Presenters need a separate, simple demo control surface.

## Main jobs

- Read the speaker's actual words while listening.
- Search and revisit an earlier passage without losing the current reading position.
- Join a presenter-created live room by code and follow finalized speech as it is released.
- Watch or listen to the shared conference source beside the current topic thread.
- Create a fresh room, share its invite link or code, prepare a source, and start the live session.

## Product tone

Calm, clear, editorial, and respectful. The media, current topic, and words sit together; controls stay quiet until needed. Explain room state plainly.

## Visual direction

Use a warm, light reading surface, dark ink, and one restrained accent. Typography should feel considered and comfortable at ordinary and enlarged sizes. Use Source Sans 3 as the first self-hosted typeface to evaluate in the rendered interface.

Avoid dashboard framing, decorative status dots, ornamental bullet points, gradients, repeated subtitles, unnecessary cards, slogan fragments, and copy that relies on forced contrasts. Preserve clear keyboard focus, screen-reader names, reduced motion, and spacing overrides.
